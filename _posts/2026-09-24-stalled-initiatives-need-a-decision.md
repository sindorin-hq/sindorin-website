When a technology programme loses momentum, the usual response is to add something: a new vendor, another workstream, more people. Most of the time, what is missing is a decision that nobody has been asked to make.

Stalled initiatives tend to look like technical problems from the inside. Integrations take longer than planned. Data is not where anyone thought it was. Users are slow to adopt. Each of these is real, but each is usually a symptom of a choice that was deferred at the start and is now being made by default, one workaround at a time.

## Four signs the problem is not the technology

1. Nobody can state, in one sentence, what the initiative is meant to change for the business.
2. Different leaders describe success in ways that cannot all be true at once.
3. The same issues return to the steering group each month without being resolved.
4. The team is waiting on an approval, a priority call or a scope decision rather than on a technical fix.

If two or more of these are true, adding capability will make the programme more expensive without making it more likely to succeed.

## Name the decision, then make it

Start by writing down the decision the initiative depends on, in one sentence. Not “improve maintenance planning”, but “should we replace the planning system, or fix the data feeding the one we have?” A decision written that plainly has a small number of credible answers, and each can be tested against value, operational reality, risk and readiness.

The answer will not always be to push on. Sometimes the right call is to narrow the scope to the part that is already working, or to pause until a dependency is resolved. What matters is that the choice is made deliberately, by the people accountable for the outcome, with the trade-offs in view.

## Connect it to the next 90 days

A decision only helps if it changes what happens on Monday. Give it an owner, the two or three priorities that follow from it, the dependencies that could stop it, and a first milestone you can measure. That is usually enough to get a stalled initiative moving again, or to stop it cleanly.
